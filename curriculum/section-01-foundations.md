# Section 1 — Foundations: Health Equity, Diagnostic Justice & the Laboratory Director

## Purpose

Establish the conceptual framework learners will use throughout the curriculum. The section should move learners from viewing the laboratory as a place that performs tests to viewing it as part of a diagnostic system that laboratory directors can measure, influence, redesign, and advocate around.

## Proposed lessons

### 1.1 — From Health Disparities to Health Equity
**Objective:** Distinguish differences, disparities, and inequities in laboratory and diagnostic data without assuming causation.

### 1.2 — What Is Laboratory Equity?
**Objective:** Explain why equitable diagnostic care requires more than making a laboratory test technically available.

### 1.3 — The Diagnostic Journey
**Objective:** Identify points across the total diagnostic process where social, clinical, laboratory, and structural factors can influence diagnostic outcomes.

### 1.4 — The Laboratory Is Not a Neutral Observer
**Objective:** Explain how selection, testing practices, and laboratory processes shape the populations represented in laboratory datasets.

### 1.5 — From Laboratory Result to Diagnostic System
**Objective:** Differentiate analytical quality, laboratory stewardship, and diagnostic stewardship.

### 1.6 — Who Is the Laboratory Responsible To?
**Objective:** Identify the major stakeholders affected by laboratory decisions and recognize potential competing interests among patients, laboratory staff, clinicians, institutions, and communities.

### 1.7 — What Can a Laboratory Director Actually Change?
**Objective:** Distinguish areas of direct control, influence, and partnership/advocacy.

### 1.8 — Evidence, Context & Director Judgment
**Objective:** Apply published evidence while recognizing local data needs, setting-specific constraints, stakeholder perspectives, and tradeoffs.

### 1.9 — Director Challenge
An unfolding case designed to test interpretation of an apparent testing disparity, denominator selection, the diagnostic journey, stakeholder identification, and control vs influence.

### 1.10 — Section Debrief
Five concise director takeaways plus curated foundational references.

## Design notes

Keep lessons short and case-based. Avoid presenting observed demographic differences as proof of inequity without investigating mechanism. Explicitly distinguish published evidence from local interpretation and educational case reasoning.
