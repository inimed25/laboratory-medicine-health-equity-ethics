# Beat 1 — You found a difference

## Screen 1A — Cold open

### On screen

**YOU FOUND A DIFFERENCE.**

During a laboratory quality review, your team examines completion of outpatient fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) after a kit is ordered for colorectal cancer screening.

| Patient population | FIT completed |
|---|---:|
| Patients living in neighborhoods with lower area-level socioeconomic deprivation | 82% |
| Patients living in neighborhoods with higher area-level socioeconomic deprivation | 64% |

The difference persists when you look across several months of data.

### Prompt

**What does this finding establish?**

### Answer choices

**A.** patients living in neighborhoods with higher area-level socioeconomic deprivation is experiencing an inequity in diagnostic care.

**B.** The difference is probably explained by biological differences between the populations.

**C.** Clinicians are providing lower-quality care to patients living in neighborhoods with higher area-level socioeconomic deprivation.

**D.** A difference in test completion has been observed, but these data alone do not establish why it exists.

### Learner action
Select one answer before continuing.

---

## Feedback

### If D — best supported

**Yes. Start with what the data actually show.**

These data establish an **observed difference in test completion** between the populations represented in this dataset.

They do not yet establish:

- why the difference occurred,
- whether the groups had the same opportunity to complete testing,
- whether the difference reflects an inequity,
- whether the relevant mechanism occurs inside or outside the laboratory, or
- whether the categories themselves are sufficiently informative for the question being asked.

**Observation ≠ explanation.**

The difference may be important. The disciplined response is not to dismiss it—or explain it prematurely—but to get curious about what produced it.

### If A

**Possible. Not established yet.**

An inequity may ultimately help explain the pattern, but an observed difference alone does not establish that conclusion. To make that determination, you need additional information about context, mechanism, opportunity, and whether the difference reflects unfair or avoidable disadvantage.

### If B

**The data do not establish a biological explanation.**

A demographic or population-level difference in an outcome does not, by itself, tell you which causal pathway produced it. Biological, clinical, social, structural, measurement, and healthcare-system processes can sometimes generate similar observed patterns.

### If C

**The data do not establish clinician behavior as the cause.**

Ordering practices could be relevant in some situations, but this particular measure begins *after the test has already been ordered*. Completion could be influenced by collection requirements, scheduling, transportation, cost, communication, specimen logistics, patient preference, documentation, or other processes.

### Persistent takeaway

> **Start with the observation. Then investigate the explanation.**

---

## Screen 1B — Get curious

### On screen

Before seeing any more data, choose **three questions** you would want answered next.

### Question bank

- Who is included in each population category?
- What exactly counts as “test completed”?
- Are there differences in where or how specimens are collected?
- Are there differences in insurance coverage or patient cost?
- Does the test require a return visit?
- Are there transportation, scheduling, language, or accessibility barriers?
- Are completion data missing differently across groups?
- Is the pattern present across clinical sites?
- What happened between ordering and collection?
- Is the difference clinically meaningful?
- What explanations have patients, clinicians, laboratory staff, or community partners identified?

### Learner action
Choose any three.

### Reveal

**There is no single required trio.**

Different questions test different possible explanations. The important move is from:

**“I see a difference, therefore I know what it means.”**

to

**“I see a difference. What information would help me understand what produced it?”**

### Director Lens

**Curiosity is not neutrality toward a potential problem.** A finding can deserve investigation before its cause is known.

# Beat 2 — Words change the claim

## Transition

You have described the result as a **difference**.

But words such as *disparity*, *inequity*, and *equity* make additional claims. Before using them interchangeably, ask:

**What am I actually saying?**

---

## Screen 2A — Match the claim

### Instructions

For each statement, choose the term that best matches what the statement is claiming.

Terms available:

**Difference / inequality**  
**Disparity**  
**Inequity**  
**Equity**

> **Important:** Terminology is not perfectly standardized. The goal is to recognize how the strength and meaning of a claim can change—not to pretend every organization uses these words identically.

---

### Statement 1

> In this dataset, test completion was 82% in patients living in neighborhoods with lower area-level socioeconomic deprivation and 64% in patients living in neighborhoods with higher area-level socioeconomic deprivation.

**Best match:** Difference / inequality

### Reveal

This is primarily a **descriptive observation**. It tells us that measured outcomes differ. It does not, by itself, explain why.

---

### Statement 2

> patients living in neighborhoods with higher area-level socioeconomic deprivation experiences a systematic difference in diagnostic access associated with social disadvantage.

**Best match:** Disparity

### Reveal

Many health-equity frameworks use **disparity** for differences linked to social, economic, environmental, or other disadvantage. However, definitions vary across organizations and publications.

**When reading the literature, check how the authors define the term.**

---

### Statement 3

> The difference in diagnostic access is linked to an avoidable barrier that unfairly limits one population's opportunity to receive appropriate testing.

**Best match:** Inequity

### Reveal

**Inequity** adds a normative judgment: the difference is not merely present; it is understood as unfair or unjust and, in many definitions, avoidable or remediable.

That conclusion requires more than observing two different numbers.

---

### Statement 4

> Patients have a fair opportunity to receive appropriate diagnostic testing and benefit from its results, with resources and processes responsive to differing needs and barriers.

**Best match:** Equity

### Reveal

**Equity is not simply identical treatment.**

Equal processes can produce unequal opportunities when people encounter different barriers or needs. Equity asks whether the system provides a fair opportunity to achieve appropriate care and outcomes.

---

## Screen 2B — One dataset, increasingly strong claims

### Visual / interaction

Show the statements as a ladder that builds one rung at a time:

**1. DIFFERENCE**  
*The measured outcomes are not the same.*

↓ adds information about pattern/context

**2. DISPARITY**  
*The difference is meaningfully connected to disadvantage under the definition being used.*

↓ adds judgment about fairness/justice and mechanism/context

**3. INEQUITY**  
*The difference reflects unfair or unjust conditions.*

↓ shifts from describing a problem toward the desired state

**4. EQUITY**  
*People have a fair opportunity to receive and benefit from appropriate care.*

### Key question

**Did the original 82% vs 64% dashboard give you enough information to climb the entire ladder?**

**No.**

The numbers gave you the first rung.

Additional evidence and context are needed before making stronger claims.

## Screen 2C — A terminology trap

### Prompt

A published paper calls a difference between two groups a **“health disparity.”** Your institution finds a superficially similar difference.

Which response is most defensible?

**A.** Call the local difference an inequity because the published study already established the mechanism.

**B.** Avoid studying the local difference because demographic comparisons are inherently misleading.

**C.** Examine how the paper defined disparity, evaluate whether its evidence and context apply locally, and investigate the local pattern before making stronger causal or normative claims.

**D.** Use the terminology from the published paper unchanged so the literature remains consistent.

### Best answer
**C**

### Feedback

Published evidence can tell you that a question may be worth asking. It does not automatically establish what is happening in another population, institution, or diagnostic system.

We will return to this problem when we reach **PLACE**.

# Beat 2 close — What changed?

At the beginning, you had two numbers.

You now have a more precise set of questions:

- **What was observed?**
- **What additional claim am I making when I call it a disparity?**
- **What evidence would support calling it an inequity?**
- **What would equity actually require in this diagnostic system?**

But we still have a problem.

The dashboard only shows what happened **inside one slice of the diagnostic process**.

To understand where a difference might come from, we need to zoom out.

> **NEXT: Where could this difference come from?**

## Writing guardrails
- Do not imply every measured difference is an inequity.
- Do not imply uncertainty means inaction or dismissal.
- Avoid presenting demographic categories as causal variables.
- Preserve definitional variation around “disparity.”
- Avoid moralized feedback such as “good/bad person” framing.
- Do not require learners to guess the author's preferred social or political conclusion.

## UX notes
- Screen 1A should appear before navigation exposes explanatory material.
- Keep the dashboard visually credible but clearly fictional.
- Screen 1B should allow multiple valid question combinations.
- Screen 2A can use tap-to-match or selection cards; always provide keyboard-accessible alternatives.
- The terminology ladder should animate only after learner attempts the classifications.
- Do not use red/green alone for incorrect/correct feedback.
