and assay coverage

Consider how intervals, eligibility thresholds, and assay targets can affect patients differently.

Step 1 of 3

Case: Who informs the reference interval?

Hypothetical case

A patient's absolute neutrophil count (ANC) is 1,400/µL, flagged below the laboratory's reference interval. Previous counts are similar. The patient has confirmed Duffy-null status, which is associated with lower circulating neutrophil counts.

During a laboratory consultation, a clinician asks whether the flag necessarily indicates a disease process.

Question 1

Which response best explains the reference-interval issue?

Choose an answer

Best answer: C. A reference interval describes a population distribution. Confirmed Duffy-null status may help explain how this stable count compares with a relevant population; race alone cannot supply that information. Neither the flag nor Duffy status replaces assessment for other causes when clinically indicated.

Evidence and limitations
  • Development of Duffy Null–Specific Absolute Neutrophil Count Reference Ranges

See the accompanying evidence note for the study or guidance scope. The teaching scenario is not a patient-specific recommendation.

Evidence and scope

Real-world basis: A 2023 study estimated a central 95% ANC reference interval of 1,210–5,390/µL from 120 Duffy-null samples in a selected adult primary-care cohort. Participants self-identified as Black or African American; the study was not a representative sample of all Duffy-null populations. This illustrates why the population informing an interval matters; it is not a universal diagnostic or treatment threshold.

Reference: Development of Duffy Null–Specific Absolute Neutrophil Count Reference Ranges. JAMA. 2023;329(23):2088–2089.

Step 2 of 3

Question 2

A research team uses a fixed minimum ANC to determine eligibility for a cancer trial. The threshold falls within the published reference interval for some healthy Duffy-null individuals.

What equity concern does this raise?

Choose an answer

Best answer: D. A uniform threshold can affect people differently when their baseline distributions differ. A reference interval describes a population distribution; it does not establish treatment safety or replace a treatment-specific assessment.

Evidence and limitations
  • Cancer Trial Eligibility and Therapy Modifications for Individuals With Duffy Null–Associated Neutrophil Count

See the accompanying evidence note for the study or guidance scope. The teaching scenario is not a patient-specific recommendation.

Evidence and scope

Real-world basis: A 2024 cross-sectional analysis examined 289 phase 3 cancer trials and 71 curative-intent anticancer regimens. It found that 76.5% of the trials had explicit or implicit eligibility restrictions affecting ANC values within the Duffy-null–associated reference range. This was an analysis of criteria, not a measurement of how many Duffy-null patients were actually excluded or a treatment-safety trial. It supports the concern about how thresholds are used, rather than a universal recommendation to lower them.

Reference: Cancer Trial Eligibility and Therapy Modifications for Individuals With Duffy Null–Associated Neutrophil Count.

Shared debrief

The first question concerns how a result is interpreted against a reference population. The second concerns how a threshold determines access. Laboratory expertise can help distinguish those uses and explain the relevant evidence to clinical and research teams.

Things to keep in mind

The reference population helps shape what gets labeled abnormal. A technically accurate measurement may be compared with an interval that does not adequately reflect a relevant biological characteristic.

Race does not establish Duffy status. A demographic category cannot substitute for the patient's known biological characteristic.

A reference interval is not a treatment-safety threshold. Being within an interval does not establish eligibility or safety for every intervention.

Question 3

Hypothetical case

A laboratory compares two targeted pharmacogenetic panels. Both accurately detect the variants they include. One tests only CYP2C9 *2 and *3; the other also includes *5, *6, *8, and *11.

A colleague asks: “If both have excellent analytical accuracy, why would the additional variants matter for equity?”

Which response best explains the concern?

Choose an answer

Best answer: C. Accuracy for included targets and the scope of those targets are different properties. Review whether the panel covers variants relevant to its intended use. Ancestry patterns can inform target selection, but do not establish an individual genotype. AMP/CAP’s cited CYP2C9 recommendation includes all six alleles in its Tier 1 minimum set.

Evidence and limitations
  • AMP/CAP — Recommendations for Clinical CYP2C9 Genotyping Allele Selection

See the accompanying evidence note for the study or guidance scope. The teaching scenario is not a patient-specific recommendation.

Evidence and scope

Real-world basis: AMP/CAP’s 2019 CYP2C9 allele-selection recommendations include *2, *3, *5, *6, *8, and *11 in the Tier 1 minimum set, considering allele function, population frequency, and availability of reference materials. The additional alleles in this case are therefore not merely optional extras in that recommendation. Both panels are hypothetical; analytical accuracy for included targets does not establish adequate target selection. This example does not independently establish the representativeness of a clinical validation cohort or recommend treatment.

Reference: AMP/CAP — Recommendations for Clinical CYP2C9 Genotyping Allele Selection.

Takeaway: What an assay leaves out can matter as much as how accurately it measures what is included.
