Practice: plan the next measurement

The team proposes a small pilot to keep collection supplies available. Which measurement plan best connects implementation, equity, and unintended burden?

Question 4

Choose an answer

Best answer: B. The process measure tests whether the intended change happened. Stratified completion shows who reached the next diagnostic step, and a balancing measure looks for burdens introduced or shifted by the change. Define denominators from eligible reactive-antibody episodes, rather than only received RNA specimens. Staff and patients can help choose feasible measures. This is a proposed local evaluation, not a validated intervention.

Evidence and limitations

This measurement plan is case-based QI reasoning. The existing CDC source supports the HCV workflow premise, not a finding that this supply pilot improves equity. Repeated measurements and local context would help interpret any change.

Bring the proposed measures to patients, staff, and clinical partners before deciding how to run the pilot.

Optional outcome reflection

Optional outcome reflection

Testing completion, successful patient contact, referrals, treatment initiation, and time to treatment describe different stages. Measures restricted to patients who reach a later stage can leave out those lost earlier in the pathway.

Participation and resources

Diverse participation can bring relevant perspectives into a QI discussion. Inclusion also involves whose perspectives influence decisions. One participant cannot represent every experience within a population. The same intervention may require different staff time, supplies, transport, or patient effort across settings.

Evidence and scope

Real-world basis

CDC’s 2023 HCV operational guidance recommends single-visit sample collection and automatic HCV RNA testing after a reactive antibody result. It defines automatic testing as occurring without additional action by the patient or provider and cites implementation reports showing improved completion. The guidance supports the workflow premise; it does not validate this case’s invented clinic-setting gaps, supply findings, or treatment outcomes. The fictional before-and-after counts do not establish causation.

Reference: CDC — Updated Operational Guidance for Implementing CDC's Recommendations on Testing for Hepatitis C Virus Infection.
