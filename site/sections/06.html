Question 1

Which consideration most directly connects the choice of testing approach to equity?

Choose an answer

Best answer: A. A test's practical suitability includes what patients and the testing system must do to obtain a usable result. Equal policy, lower direct cost, or a single collection visit does not establish equivalent access.

Question 2

The team favors a blood-based test because it avoids a return visit and is not affected by prior BCG vaccination. What limits that reasoning in the proposed workflow?

Choose an answer

Best answer: B. Patient-facing advantages can be lost if the workflow cannot reliably produce a valid result. Tests for TB infection do not, by themselves, distinguish latent infection from active disease.

Real-world basis: CDC guidance describes the benefits of TB blood tests for BCG-vaccinated patients and patients unlikely to return for skin-test reading, alongside specimen-processing requirements and limitations. The case's specific clinic logistics are hypothetical.

Takeaway: A test's practical suitability depends on the population and testing setting.

Things to keep in mind

Lower direct cost does not automatically mean lower patient burden. Repeat travel, time away from work, and incomplete testing also matter.

Operational constraints are contextual. Staffing, transport, and processing capacity can change.

Clinical usefulness extends beyond analytical performance. A test must be accessible and capable of producing information that can be used in care.

References: CDC — Clinical Testing Guidance for TB: Interferon Gamma Release Assay and CDC — Clinical Testing Guidance for TB: Tuberculin Skin Test. Product labeling and receiving-laboratory instructions determine the handling requirements for the specific collection and testing workflow.
