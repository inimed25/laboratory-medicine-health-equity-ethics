Access to diagnostic characterization and its timing

A 2022 observational study found that, among patients with advanced nonsquamous non–small-cell lung cancer, 29.7% of Black patients versus 36.6% of White patients received NGS testing before first-line therapy.

Question 1

A colleague asks, “But both groups received a cancer diagnosis—what diagnostic disparity remains?” Which response best captures the concern?

Choose an answer

Best answer: D. A diagnosis can correctly name the disease while leaving clinically relevant features uncharacterized. Here, the documented disparity concerns receipt of molecular testing before treatment—not demonstrated differences in diagnostic accuracy or the prevalence of actionable alterations. The testing measure does not establish when a completed report became available.

Question 2

The study also reported lower NGS testing rates among Black patients at any time during the study, not only before first-line therapy: 43.8% versus 54.7% among White patients.

Why does distinguishing “before first-line therapy” from “at any time” matter?

Choose an answer

Best answer: B. Eventually receiving testing is different from having molecular information available at an earlier decision point. An “ever tested” measure can miss unequal opportunities to use information when initial treatment decisions are made. Neither measure alone establishes why testing was absent or delayed, whether results were available when needed, or how treatment outcomes were affected.

Shared takeaway

A diagnosis can be established while access to clinically relevant characterization remains unequal. Access also has a timing dimension: whether testing occurs and when its information becomes available describe different aspects of care.

Access context: Insurance coverage, authorization requirements, and patient cost can be relevant questions when testing is not received. They are not established explanations for the racial differences shown here. A testing-rate comparison alone cannot distinguish financial barriers from ordering, specimen availability, clinical context, or other mechanisms.

Real-world evidence and case boundaries

Real-world basis

The quoted rates come from a retrospective observational analysis of a real-world deidentified database. They document differences in testing receipt in the studied population and period. They do not establish that every untested patient had an actionable alteration, that testing would have changed every treatment decision, or that the observed gap has the same size in every setting today. The colleague's question is an educational framing, not a quoted study exchange.

Reference: Disparities in Biomarker Testing and Clinical Trial Enrollment Among Patients With Lung, Breast, or Colorectal Cancers in the United States. JCO Precision Oncology. 2022;6:e2100427.
