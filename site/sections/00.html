Health equity in laboratory medicine

A case-based space for learning, analysis, and discussion among pathology residents and fellows.

Why this module?

Laboratory testing shapes opportunities for diagnosis and care. This module explores how access, testing choices, reference populations, reporting, and data can relate to health and health care disparities.

The purpose is to deepen interpretation and invite questions—not to present a single correct implementation plan for every setting.

Learning objectives

By the end, learners should be better able to:

  • Distinguish health disparities, health care disparities, health inequity, and social determinants of health.
  • Recognize how laboratory workflows and testing decisions can create or reinforce unequal opportunities for care.
  • Interpret case findings alongside published evidence, including what each does and does not establish.
  • Discuss how pathologists can help clinicians understand test utility, limitations, and the implications of laboratory data.

About the author and AI assistance

This module was developed by a clinical pathology resident exploring how laboratory medicine relates to health equity. It is a learning project intended to support critical analysis, discussion, and further inquiry. Feedback from learners and colleagues is welcome.

AI tools assisted with drafting, editing, organizing the material, and building the site. The content may contain errors or omissions; references are provided so readers can explore the evidence and evaluate the interpretations.

Understanding the cases and evidence

Cases, patients, local workflows, and numerical examples are fictional unless explicitly identified as published findings with a citation.

Explanations draw on studies, professional guidance, and conceptual sources. References support the accompanying claims, rather than every detail of a fictional scenario. Findings should be understood within the populations and settings studied.

Corresponding references appear in answer explanations and case evidence notes, with optional further reading at the end.

This educational beta does not replace current guidance or patient-specific professional judgment. Corrections, alternative interpretations, and thoughtful critique are welcome. Send feedback or report a correction on GitHub (GitHub account required). Include the section and question, the issue, and a supporting source if available. This is a public issue tracker; do not include patient information or private details.

Using the module

Choose an answer, reveal the explanation, and explore the evidence. There is no timer or passing score. The Sections menu lets you move between topics; progress and answers are saved in this browser when storage is available. Use “Reset progress” to clear them. No answers are sent to a server.
